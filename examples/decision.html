Whether Wheelhouse pays the instructor

Two ways to end a settlement, and what each one puts on us.

The settlement statement ends with a button. What that button does is yours to pick.

The statement is built. It totals what an instructor earned in a week of classes and ends in one action. Today that action marks the money as paid — a record that someone, somewhere, sent it. The money itself never touches Wheelhouse.

That split was decided in the align interview on 29 June: phase one ships the record only, and the mockups leave a clean seam where the second version slots in.

The other version sends it. Same statement, same button, same finished state — but the instructor picks PayPal, Venmo, CashApp or their bank, and the money leaves through us.

Sending money is not one feature. It is four.

The button is the smallest part. Everything under it has to exist first, and none of it does.

Onboard the instructor
Name, address, tax details, and one payout method they can change later. Nobody has been asked for any of this yet.
Wire up a provider
One integration reaching all four rails. The drawn version assumes a card processor's transfer product, which reaches one — a bank account.
Carry the tax obligation
Once money moves through us, year-end reporting is ours. This is the step that decides the answer.
Handle the failures
Bounced transfers, closed accounts, someone who left the country. Support that has never been staffed.
The amber step is not a build problem. It is the one that changes what Wheelhouse is — a booking tool that keeps records, or a business that moves other people's money.
