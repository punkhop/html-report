How a page gets made

One question, a shelf of blocks, and the rules that pick between them.

Say it out loud, name the job, shop the shelf, build, look at it.

Five steps, in order, every time you ask for a page. The whole system is the middle three — the first stops essays, the last catches what only eyes catch.

Say it
The whole thing in plain sentences, across a desk. A page that cannot be said out loud turns into an essay.
Name the job
What do you have to DO with this page? That is the job, not how much of the page each part fills.
Shop the shelf
Pick blocks by what the content IS. Every block says what it is for and what it must never hold.
Build
One stylesheet, no libraries. Every page links it, so changing the center changes them all.
Look at it
Screenshot the real render at desk width and phone width before you ever see it.
The arrows above are a claim: first this, then that. That is the only reason they are there. Five things with no order would be a grid.

Every page starts with one question: what am I presenting to you?

Six answers, and the answer is whichever one names what you have to do. If you have to answer something, it is a decision however long the teaching runs. If you only have to understand, it is an explainer however many numbers appear.

Most pages are two of these at once. The dominant job sets the order and the opening; the second contributes blocks into it. The page answers once — never twice, never switching format halfway down.

Decision
“Decide this with me.” Opens with the choices side by side, closes with my call.
Mission map
“So I don't lose track.” Opens with the count, carries the rail of what is done, doing and next.
Findings
“Go find out and tell me what to do.” Opens with the winner, then what is wrong with it.
Proof
“Show me it works.” Opens with the tally, then a capture beside every claim.
Explainer
“Help me understand.” Teaches top to bottom, one metaphor carried through. The craft job.
The thing itself
The subject is a screen, so the page IS the screen. A described surface is an unfinished page.

Blocks are tagged by where they are at home, not fenced in.

Every block in the catalog carries the kinds it belongs to. A decision page starts from the decision blocks — but a decision block goes on an explainer the moment that explainer leaves you a choice, and a capture goes on a findings page the moment a claim needs proving.

So two decision pages are never identical. What is fixed is the skeleton and the rules. What varies is which blocks the content asks for.

One thing is never allowed: a block chosen because it exists.

Say the relationship aloud, and check the shape is saying it.

Every arrangement makes a claim about how its items relate. Arrows claim order. A rail claims something happened. Nesting claims containment. Read the claim out loud, and if the sentence is a lie, the shape is wrong — however good it looks.
